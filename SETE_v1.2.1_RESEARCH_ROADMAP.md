# RESEARCH ROADMAP — SETE v1.2.1 → FUTURE VALIDATION

**Project:** From Sunrise to Our Genome — Can We Still Hear the Music — Smart Evolutionary Tourism Ecosystem (SETE): Metacognitive Challenge Triage, Responsible Continuity, and Evolution Before Perfection  
**Version:** 1.2.1  
**DOI:** 10.5281/zenodo.23068992

## 1. Strategic Objective

Develop SETE from a conceptual architecture into a progressively validated research ecosystem without requiring complete infrastructure before useful intelligence can be tested.

## 2. Roadmap

### Phase 0 — Concept Stabilization
**Objective:** freeze definitions and prevent concept proliferation.

Outputs:
- ontology;
- terminology;
- boundary conditions;
- novelty map;
- literature review;
- version-controlled architecture.

Gate:
**Conceptually coherent?**

### Phase 1 — Data Architecture
**Objective:** establish a Minimum Viable Tourism Intelligence System (MVTIS).

Data:
- open tourism statistics;
- GIS;
- environmental indicators;
- mobility;
- destination characteristics;
- community observations;
- visitor observations.

Gate:
**Sufficient provenance and uncertainty representation?**

### Phase 2 — Destination Capability Mapping
**Objective:** create dynamic capability profiles.

Potential dimensions:
- infrastructure;
- environmental capacity;
- mobility;
- accommodation;
- cultural assets;
- community capacity;
- seasonal variation.

Gate:
**Can capability be represented dynamically?**

### Phase 3 — Tourist Preference Spectrum
**Objective:** model heterogeneous tourist preferences without reducing diversity to simplistic categories.

Methods:
- clustering;
- latent-variable methods;
- preference learning;
- longitudinal observation.

Gate:
**Does the model preserve meaningful heterogeneity?**

### Phase 4 — Matching Engine
**Objective:** match tourist preference profiles with destination capability.

Constraints:
- capacity;
- ecology;
- community;
- time;
- mobility;
- accessibility;
- uncertainty.

Gate:
**Does matching improve defined outcomes against baselines?**

### Phase 5 — Spatial and Temporal Load Balancing
**Objective:** test whether tourism pressure can be redistributed responsibly.

Measures:
- peak congestion;
- spatial concentration;
- temporal concentration;
- environmental pressure;
- resident burden.

Gate:
**Does redistribution reduce net pressure rather than merely move it?**

### Phase 6 — HMC Challenge Triage
**Objective:** operationalize obstacle classification.

Pipeline:

OBSTACLE
→ FAILURE CONDITION
→ EVIDENCE REQUIREMENT
→ TEST
→ ACCEPTANCE CRITERION
→ MITIGATION
→ RESIDUAL RISK
→ REVIEW
→ DECISION

Gate:
**Does triage reduce avoidable delay without weakening safeguards?**

### Phase 7 — Dual Risk Accounting
**Objective:** explicitly compare:

**RISK OF ACTION ↔ RISK OF INACTION**

Gate:
**Does explicit inaction risk improve decision quality?**

### Phase 8 — LOOPTIMA / Exit Before Optimization
**Objective:** detect technical over-optimization.

Questions:
- Is the subsystem still relevant?
- Does complexity improve outcomes?
- Can the subsystem be removed?
- Has the central objective disappeared?

Gate:
**Is additional optimization justified?**

### Phase 9 — Community and Governance Validation
**Objective:** ensure optimization does not override legitimate community interests.

Components:
- participation;
- consent where applicable;
- privacy;
- transparency;
- accountability;
- independent review.

Gate:
**Is the system socially and institutionally legitimate in the tested context?**

### Phase 10 — Nature and Wellbeing Validation
**Objective:** test ecological and human outcomes.

Potential indicators:
- biodiversity;
- habitat pressure;
- resource consumption;
- resident wellbeing;
- visitor wellbeing;
- local economic distribution.

Gate:
**Are positive outcomes robust and unintended harms controlled?**

### Phase 11 — Cross-Destination Interoperability
**Objective:** connect multiple local systems.

Architecture:

LOCAL
→ REGIONAL
→ CROSS-DESTINATION
→ GLOBAL CAPABILITY MAP
→ GLOBAL ADAPTIVE COORDINATION

Gate:
**Does interoperability produce net value greater than coordination cost?**

### Phase 12 — Longitudinal Validation
**Objective:** test whether SETE actually learns and adapts over time.

Measure:
- performance drift;
- adaptation;
- resilience;
- seasonal change;
- policy changes;
- ecological change;
- behavioural change.

Gate:
**Does the system improve through learning rather than merely accumulate complexity?**

## 3. Research Decision Logic

Every major development should pass four complementary gates:

### Gate 1 — HMC Challenge Gate
What is the obstacle?

### Gate 2 — LOOPTIMA Gate
Are we trapped in local optimization?

### Gate 3 — HMH Gate
Is optimization aligned with human and ecological objectives?

### Gate 4 — Safety & Governance Gate
Are legitimate safeguards satisfied?

Possible outputs:

**CONTINUE / STOP / REDIRECT / RESTRUCTURE / PILOT**

## 4. Core Development Rule

> **Evolution Before Perfection.**

And:

> **The project does not need to be complete before it becomes useful.**

But equally:

> **Useful does not mean validated.**

## 5. Success Criteria

SETE should not be considered successful merely because the software works.

Success requires evidence that the architecture improves defined outcomes while controlling:

- ecological harm;
- community burden;
- inequality;
- privacy risks;
- governance failures;
- algorithmic error;
- infrastructure overload;
- unnecessary complexity.

## 6. Failure and Exit Criteria

The project should pause, redirect or terminate a component if:

- evidence shows no meaningful improvement;
- simpler baselines outperform it;
- costs exceed benefits;
- unintended harms dominate;
- community legitimacy cannot be established;
- ecological constraints are repeatedly violated;
- data quality makes conclusions unreliable;
- metacognitive triage becomes a source of unjustified action;
- or the architecture becomes an optimization trap.

## 7. Long-Term Vision

The long-term research question is:

> **Can tourism become an adaptive global intelligence ecosystem that continuously observes itself, learns from heterogeneous destinations and tourists, manages capacity and sustainability constraints, recognizes its own implementation limitations, and evolves without sacrificing human agency or ecological integrity?**

This remains an open empirical research question.
