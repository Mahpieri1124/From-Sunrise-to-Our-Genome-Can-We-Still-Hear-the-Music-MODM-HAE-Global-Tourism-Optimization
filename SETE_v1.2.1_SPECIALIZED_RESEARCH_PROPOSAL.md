## 1. Executive Summary

This proposal seeks to operationalize SETE as a progressively validated tourism intelligence architecture capable of coordinating heterogeneous tourist preferences with dynamic destination capability while explicitly managing ecological, infrastructural, social, cultural and governance constraints.

The distinctive research direction is the integration of **Metacognitive Challenge Triage**: implementation obstacles become objects of structured analysis rather than automatic stopping conditions.

## 2. Core Hypothesis

A globally interconnected tourism architecture may achieve more adaptive and potentially more sustainable coordination when it:

1. models tourist heterogeneity;
2. models destination capability dynamically;
3. matches demand and capacity;
4. distributes tourism spatially and temporally;
5. incorporates community and ecological constraints;
6. monitors outcomes continuously;
7. explicitly models both action and inaction risk;
8. classifies implementation obstacles before treating them as stopping conditions.

## 3. Secondary Hypotheses

**H1 — Preference–Capability Matching:** dynamic matching can improve alignment between tourist preferences and destination capability.

**H2 — Load Balancing:** adaptive spatial-temporal allocation can reduce selected forms of concentration under specified capacity constraints.

**H3 — MVTIS Feasibility:** useful decision support can be generated from combinations of open/public/local data before complete infrastructure deployment.

**H4 — Challenge Triage:** structured obstacle classification can reduce avoidable delay without weakening defined safeguards.

**H5 — Dual Risk Accounting:** modelling risk of inaction changes tourism planning decisions relative to action-only models.

**H6 — Evolutionary Continuity:** progressive deployment can generate useful intermediate capabilities before full ecosystem completion.

**H7 — Community Protection:** explicit community constraints can reduce the risk that optimization improves visitor outcomes while degrading resident outcomes.

These are hypotheses, not findings.

## 5. Proposed Objective Function

A generalized multi-objective formulation may be expressed as:

**maximize:**  
Tourist–Destination Fit + Local Value + Wellbeing + Cultural Value + Resilience

**minimize:**  
Ecological Pressure + Congestion + Infrastructure Overload + Inequality + Unnecessary Travel Burden

subject to:

- ecological capacity;
- infrastructure capacity;
- community constraints;
- legal constraints;
- privacy;
- safety;
- data-quality thresholds;
- governance constraints.

The exact mathematical specification should be developed empirically rather than fixed prematurely.

## 6. Challenge-Triage Module

For every significant obstacle:

**OBJECTION / LIMITATION**  
→ FAILURE CONDITION  
→ EVIDENCE REQUIREMENT  
→ TEST  
→ ACCEPTANCE CRITERION  
→ MITIGATION  
→ RESIDUAL RISK  
→ INDEPENDENT REVIEW  
→ DECISION

The module should produce one of:

**CONTINUE / STOP / REDIRECT / RESTRUCTURE / PILOT**

## 7. Experimental Design

### Stage A — Synthetic Benchmarking
Compare SETE-inspired allocation against baseline recommendation and optimization strategies.

### Stage B — Open-Data Pilot
Use publicly available tourism, mobility, environmental and geospatial data.

### Stage C — Destination Case Studies
Select destinations with contrasting tourism pressure, infrastructure and ecological characteristics.

### Stage D — Comparative Multi-Destination Study
Test interoperability and transferability.

### Stage E — Longitudinal Evaluation
Measure adaptation over multiple tourism cycles.

## 10. Falsification Strategy

The architecture should not be protected from negative findings.

The project should be considered unsuccessful in a given context if a simpler baseline repeatedly produces equal or better outcomes with lower cost, lower complexity and fewer unintended consequences.

Similarly, if challenge triage increases harmful delay or bypasses necessary safeguards, the relevant module should be redesigned or rejected.

## 13. Core Principle

> **The objective is not to eliminate every reason for delay. The objective is to prevent avoidable or unjustified delay from becoming a structural substitute for problem-solving, while preserving legitimate safeguards.**
