## 1. Purpose

This document distinguishes between:

- established literature;
- closely related literature;
- semi-related conceptual fields;
- and the proposed SETE integration.

The objective is not to claim that SETE invented smart tourism, AI tourism, optimization, adaptive systems or sustainability.

## 3. Closest Literature

Bingöl and Yang (2025) are particularly close because they explicitly examine smart technologies and AI as components of smart destination ecosystems and develop a model for smart destination management. SETE overlaps strongly with this literature at the ecosystem and destination-management level. The distinction proposed here is the broader cross-destination/global evolutionary architecture and explicit metacognitive management of implementation challenges. citeturn0search1

Recent systematic review work similarly identifies economic, sociocultural, environmental, managerial, political and tourism-informatics dimensions of smart tourism destinations. SETE should therefore not claim to be the first integrated smart-tourism framework. citeturn0search3

## 4. Optimization Literature

Recent research already combines personalization, sustainability, uncertainty and evolutionary optimization. Aliahmadi et al. use a self-adaptive evolutionary algorithm for sustainable personalized trip planning, while Pitakaso et al. explicitly balance tourist preferences with sustainability considerations. citeturn0search2turn0search4

SETE's proposed distinction is therefore not a new optimization algorithm. It is the placement of optimization inside a broader architecture involving destination capability, global interconnectedness, community participation, metacognitive challenge triage and progressive validation.

## 5. Nature-Positive Tourism

The 2026 npj Biodiversity perspective emphasizes biodiversity conservation, habitat protection, behaviour change and minimizing environmental impacts in nature-positive tourism. citeturn0search0

SETE incorporates this direction rather than claiming to originate it.

## 6. Human–AI Control

Research on human control of AI distinguishes supervisory control from human–machine teaming. citeturn0search12

SETE's HMC layer is conceptually related but tourism-specific: it is intended to determine whether an obstacle in the research/deployment process is a genuine stopping condition, a solvable engineering problem, an information problem, a governance issue, or a local optimization trap.

## 7. The Defensible Novelty Claim

The most defensible formulation is:

> **SETE v1.2.1 proposes an integrative, globally oriented, evolutionary tourism architecture in which tourist heterogeneity, destination capability, adaptive matching, spatial-temporal load balancing, sustainability, community participation and progressive validation are coupled with a metacognitive challenge-triage mechanism designed to distinguish legitimate stopping conditions from constraints that may be progressively addressed through system evolution.**

This is stronger academically than claiming that SETE invented each component.

## 8. What Would Count as Genuine Future Novelty?

The novelty claim could become substantially stronger if future empirical work demonstrates:

1. measurable reduction in avoidable implementation delay;
2. improved outcomes over simpler tourism-management baselines;
3. reliable global interoperability across heterogeneous destinations;
4. measurable benefits from explicit action/inaction risk modelling;
5. validated effectiveness of the HMC Challenge Gate;
6. demonstrable reduction of harmful tourism concentration without displacement;
7. community and ecological outcomes that remain positive under optimization;
8. reproducible performance across multiple regions.

Until then, the contribution remains **conceptual and hypothesis-generating**.
