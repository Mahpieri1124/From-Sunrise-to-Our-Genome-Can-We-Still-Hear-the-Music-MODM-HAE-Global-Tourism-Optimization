# SETE Version 1.2.4 — GLOBAL NOVELTY & PRIOR-ART COMPARISON

**Author:** Mohammad Piran

## Executive Finding

The current literature does **not** support claiming that “local quality of life + nature” is individually novel.

These are established research domains:

- resident quality of life;
- community wellbeing;
- community-based tourism;
- sustainable tourism;
- biodiversity/environmental impacts;
- carrying capacity;
- smart tourism;
- AI tourism;
- multi-objective optimisation;
- adaptive governance.

The most defensible novelty claim for Version 1.2.4 is therefore the **specific architectural integration** of:

> **SMDG-Clustering + Practical Multi-Layer Blind Spots + One-Shot-Two-Goal + HMC-GATE + Evolution Before Perfection**

inside the evolving SETE global tourism architecture.

This is a **novelty hypothesis**, not a verified “first in the world” claim.

## 1. Tourism Economics

Nunkoo et al. analysed 545 estimates from 113 studies. Their meta-regression supports tourism-led growth but identifies publication bias and sensitivity to country data, specifications and estimation. [1]

**SETE implication:** the economic premise is literature-grounded, but successful tourism economies do not prove the SETE architecture.

## 2. Smart Tourism Ecosystems

Gretzel et al. established the Smart Tourism Ecosystem concept in 2015. [2]

A 2026 bibliometric study analysed 352 Scopus-indexed articles and described economic, environmental, sociocultural, technological and governance dimensions of ecosystem maturity. [3]

**SETE implication:** smart tourism ecosystem itself is not claimed as new.

## 3. Smart Tourist Destinations

A 2026 review identified 15 smart-destination research areas and emphasised smart governance, smart sustainability, smart people, smart mobility and value co-creation at micro/meso/macro levels. [4]

**SETE implication:** “smart + sustainable + governance” is already strongly represented. The proposed difference is the explicit goal-cluster/blind-spot architecture.

## 4. Resident Quality of Life

A systematic review found both positive and negative effects of tourism on local QoL, including economic and employment benefits alongside possible health, safety, environmental, cost-of-living and social harms. [5]

Research on resident QoL has become a substantial systematic literature domain. [6]

**SETE implication:** Goal 1 is strongly established; novelty is in its operational integration.

## 5. Community-Based Tourism

A 2025 review analysed 847 documents and 69 systematically reviewed studies, highlighting participation, empowerment, collaboration and sustainability, while identifying fragmentation and geographical imbalance. [7]

A 2026 review of 63 studies highlights empowerment, governance, wellbeing, resilience and digital innovation, while noting unequal benefit distribution and limited local authority. [8]

**SETE implication:** local benefit is established; the proposed contribution is to place it inside an adaptive global coordination architecture.

## 6. Nature and Environmental Impacts

A 2025 literature mapping synthesised 47 interdisciplinary works and identified 24 tourism impacts across environmental, social and economic categories; biodiversity change was a prominent concern. [9]

A systematic review of 152 protected-area studies found diverse approaches to valuing nature and increasing attention to socio-cultural valuation. [10]

**SETE implication:** Goal 2 is established; the proposed contribution is simultaneous joint governance with local QoL.

## 7. Multi-Objective Optimisation

A 2023 study explicitly used an enhanced NSGA-II approach to optimise urban tourism environmental carrying capacity. [11]

**SETE implication:** multi-objective tourism optimisation is not claimed as novel. The proposed extension is:

**multi-objective optimisation + goal clustering + blind-spot detection + HMC-GATE + global adaptive coordination.**

## 8. Adaptive Governance

Hartman applies complex adaptive systems theory to tourism destination governance and emphasises adaptation and time. [12]

**SETE implication:** “tourism is adaptive” is prior art. SETE proposes to add cross-layer blind-spot review and global coordination.

## 9. Global Tourism Network

A 2022 network study constructed the Global Tourism System from UNWTO data and found high clustering, small-world properties and signs of complex adaptive behaviour. [13]

**SETE implication:** SETE does not claim to discover the global tourism network. It proposes moving from network analysis toward adaptive coordination.

## 10. AI Tourism

A 2026 systematic review examined 75 Scopus-indexed AI tourism recommendation studies from 2009–2025. [14]

A 2026 review maps the development of AI in smart tourism. [15]

A 2026 critical review examines AI governance and managerial implications for sustainable tourism. [16]

**SETE implication:** AI is not the innovation. The proposed distinction is the placement of AI under human metacognitive governance.

## 11. Green + Smart Tourism

A 2026 systematic review of 51 studies identified technology and finance as recurrent barriers and governance quality, policy coherence and institutional coordination as recurrent enablers. [17]

A 2026 smart-tourism sustainability review reports inadequate representation of SDGs in the literature. [18]

**SETE implication:** the field is moving toward the same multidimensional problem; this strengthens relevance but requires precise novelty boundaries.

## 12. Community Participation

A 2025 systematic review of 59 studies found participation in destination governance frequently remains tokenistic. [19]

**SETE implication:** if local QoL is a primary goal, residents should be treated as governance participants rather than merely measurement subjects.

# Novelty Decomposition

### Axis A — Goal as Cluster Object
Existing: tourists/destinations/behaviours are clustered.  
Proposed: goals themselves become structured objects of clustering and reassessment.  
**Status:** Proposed construct.

### Axis B — Cross-Layer Blind Spot
Existing: tourism impacts are studied across many dimensions.  
Proposed: the system explicitly searches for divergences between layers during the optimisation loop.  
**Status:** Proposed tourism-specific architecture.

### Axis C — One-Shot-Two-Goal
Existing: sustainable tourism is multidimensional.  
Proposed: Cluster (-6) deliberately makes local QoL + clean sustainable nature the immediate primary pair.  
**Status:** Design innovation, not invention of the underlying goals.

### Axis D — HMC-GATE Review
Existing: human metacognition, AI governance and adaptive governance exist as research domains.  
Proposed: second-order review of whether tourism optimisation is optimising the right goals and observing the right layers.  
**Status:** Proposed construct/integration.

### Axis E — Evolution Before Perfection
Existing: iterative/adaptive approaches are widespread.  
Proposed: explicit integration with MCT/TCND in SETE implementation.  
**Status:** Author-defined methodological integration.

# Strongest Defensible Novelty Statement

> **Version 1.2.4 proposes SMDG-Clustering as a goal-centric extension of SETE clustering logic and Practical Multi-Layer Blind Spots as a mechanism for detecting cross-layer divergence between local human and environmental outcomes. These are integrated into an explicit One-Shot-Two-Goal architecture and subjected to HMC-GATE reassessment within the Evolution Before Perfection methodology.**

# Final Verdict

### Established
Tourism economics, resident QoL, community tourism, sustainable tourism, environmental conservation, smart tourism, AI tourism, optimisation, adaptive governance and global tourism network analysis.

### Proposed SETE integration
**SMDG-Clustering + PMLBS + One-Shot-Two-Goal + HMC-GATE + Evolution Before Perfection.**

### What must still be demonstrated
1. SMDG-Clustering improves decisions.
2. PMLBS detects consequential blind spots.
3. HMC-GATE improves second-order governance.
4. Joint local/nature outcomes can be improved.
5. The architecture remains useful under incomplete data.
6. The mechanism scales beyond a single destination.

### Priority warning

Before any claim such as “first,” “unique,” or “unprecedented,” conduct a reproducible search across Scopus, Web of Science, Google Scholar, citation chains, patent databases where relevant, and software/repository sources.
