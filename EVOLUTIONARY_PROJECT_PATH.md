## 1. Purpose

This file records the evolutionary logic of the SETE programme.

The project treats **evolution itself as a methodological principle**.

A repository version is not merely a newer document. It is a new research state in which:

- concepts may be refined;
- assumptions may be challenged;
- evidence may strengthen or weaken claims;
- prior art may narrow novelty;
- models may replace verbal descriptions;
- empirical observations may alter the architecture.

The project therefore rejects silent conceptual drift.

---

# 2. Evolutionary Rule

Every major research step should answer:

1. What existed before?
2. What was added?
3. Why was it added?
4. What evidence supports it?
5. What remains uncertain?
6. What could falsify it?
7. What changes in the next version?

# 5. Evolution of the Core Innovation

## Stage 1 — Tourism has economic potential

The literature provides substantial evidence that tourism can be associated with economic growth, although effects vary by context and methodology.

**State:** Literature-supported.

---

## Stage 2 — Tourism can be strategically developed

Countries and destinations have incorporated tourism into broader economic, regional and sustainability strategies.

**State:** Literature-supported.

**Important limitation:** strategic success cannot be attributed to tourism alone.

---

## Stage 3 — Tourism is an ecosystem

Smart tourism research already conceptualises interconnected actors, technologies, data and value creation.

**State:** Established literature domain.

---

## Stage 4 — Tourism is a network

Tourist flows connect origins, destinations, businesses, infrastructures and institutions.

**State:** Established/emerging research domain.

---

## Stage 5 — Global interconnected tourism coordination

SETE proposes moving from observing the network toward investigating **adaptive coordination across the network**.

**State:** Proposed research direction.

---

## Stage 6 — Tourist Preference Spectrum

Instead of treating tourists only as fixed demographic or categorical groups, SETE proposes a multidimensional and dynamic representation of preferences.

**State:** Proposed construct requiring comparison and testing against segmentation/recommender literature.

---

## Stage 7 — Dynamic Clustering

Clusters may evolve with:

- preferences;
- context;
- season;
- destination conditions;
- prices;
- environmental conditions.

**State:** Proposed SETE implementation principle.

---

## Stage 8 — Destination Capability Mapping

A destination is represented by capabilities and constraints, not only attractions.

**State:** Proposed integrative representation.

---

## Stage 9 — Tourist–Destination–Time Matching

Matching is explicitly conditioned on temporal and capacity state.

**State:** Proposed integrative architecture.

---

## Stage 10 — Global Capacity Balancing

Tourism demand is considered as a network allocation problem rather than only an arrival-maximisation problem.

**State:** Proposed systems-level extension requiring modelling.

---

## Stage 11 — Multi-Objective Optimisation

Economic, social, cultural, ecological and resilience objectives are treated jointly.

**State:** Established method; proposed SETE integration.

---

## Stage 12 — Adaptive Feedback

Observed outcomes modify the next cycle.

**State:** Established systems principle; proposed global tourism integration.

---

## Stage 13 — AI-Assisted Intelligence

AI supports:

- clustering;
- forecasting;
- pattern recognition;
- optimisation;
- scenario generation.

**State:** Emerging/established application domain.

---

## Stage 14 — Human Metacognitive Governance

The system evaluates not only outputs but the assumptions and objectives behind those outputs.

**State:** Proposed SETE governance layer.

---

## Stage 15 — HMC-GATE

**Human Meta-Cognitive Gate** is defined as a proposed second-order governance mechanism.

**State:** Proposed construct; novelty requires systematic prior-art review.

# 10. What Must Be Tested

The next research generations should test:

1. Does dynamic preference modelling improve recommendations?
2. Does adaptive clustering outperform static segmentation under changing conditions?
3. Does destination capability mapping improve matching?
4. Does time-aware matching reduce congestion?
5. Does cross-destination balancing improve economic distribution?
6. Can ecological and social constraints be incorporated without destroying economic utility?
7. Does multi-objective optimisation outperform single-objective tourism growth strategies under defined criteria?
8. Does AI improve decision quality?
9. Does HMC-GATE improve human oversight and metacognitive calibration?
10. Does the architecture scale without unacceptable centralisation or loss of local autonomy?

---

# 11. Failure Is Part of Evolution

A failed experiment is not a failure of the research programme.

It is a mechanism for evolutionary correction.

For example:

**If dynamic clustering fails → revise representation.**

**If matching fails → revise capability model.**

**If optimisation fails → revise objectives/constraints.**

**If AI fails → reduce automation.**

**If HMC-GATE fails → revise governance mechanism.**

**If global coordination fails → narrow the scope.**

This is how a pre-empirical architecture becomes progressively evidence-based.
